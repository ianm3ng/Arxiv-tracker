Can Language Models Play the Wikipedia Game?
Authors: Alexander Hickey, Joanna Knight, James Bishop
First: 2026-10-09T17:59:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:56+00:00
Comments: 64 pages (14-page main text, references and 16 appendices), 17 figures, 10 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language models increasingly support or automate information retrieval from document collections too large to index. This task is difficult when it requires specialist linguistic or scientific knowledge. We used the Wikipedia Game to test language models on an analogous information retrieval task. In the game, players must reach a target article using only article links. We compared sentence embedding models and LLMs across five Wikipedia languages and five academic-literature datasets. We construct very large local graphs of Wikipedia pages and of paper citations using OpenAlex and provide code to build and navigate them. This allows the study of language models' navigation capabilities without relying on third-party APIs. We compared the models with human and TF-IDF benchmarks. The Wikipedia Game format enables robust comparisons across techniques, languages, and domains. No model significantly outperformed humans, although the best models (GPT-o3 mini, GPT-4o, QwQ:32B, and DeepSeek-R1:70B) achieved comparable success. Performance and rankings varied across languages and topics, with implications for practitioners utilising these techniques.

InstantFusion: A Shared Latent Interface for Heterogeneous Image Generators
Authors: Xuanlang Dai, Jiazi Bu, Yujie Zhou, Yuhong Liu, Beichen Zhang, Penghui Yang, Ziyu Liu, Yuhang Zang
First: 2026-10-09T17:59:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:04+00:00

Abstract
Diffusion/flow models have emerged as the de facto paradigms for image generation, giving rise to a diverse family of modern generators with increasingly heterogeneous architectures and representations. Despite their remarkable generation capabilities, the cross-model alignment of latent representations and the consistency of their underlying denoising dynamics remain underexplored. In this work, we uncover an intriguing cross-model compatibility: independently trained generators exhibit similar latent spatial organization, while directly handing off intermediate states between unaligned models can still preserve coarse visual content. Motivated by these observations, we propose InstantFusion, a lightweight shared latent interface that connects the denoising trajectories of heterogeneous image generators. Specifically, to enable flexible cross-model state translation, model-specific Latent AutoEncoders (LAEs) are introduced to project intermediate states from different generators into a shared latent space and decode them into the latent space of a target generator. This shared space is constrained by two complementary objectives: aligning intermediate states derived from the same image at matched noise levels and synchronizing their corresponding local denoising velocities. Once trained, InstantFusion allows denoising to seamlessly transition across frozen generators without requiring a dedicated mapping for every model pair. The versatility of InstantFusion is demonstrated across acceleration, multi-preference composition, cross-architecture on-policy distillation, and cross-model composition, achieving competitive performance across these diverse settings.

Measuring Harness-Induced Belief Divergence in Multi-Step LLM Agents
Authors: Haiwen Yi, Xinyuan Song
First: 2026-07-05T22:22:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:59:01+00:00
Comments: 15 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Software agents are becoming increasingly important in modern coding workflows, where they solve complex tasks through multi-step interaction with code repositories. A central component of these systems is the execution harness, which governs how the underlying LLM observes, acts on, and verifies its environment. We show that the harness can in turn shape the agent's evolving beliefs about the environment, even when the task, environment, and base LLM remain fixed. To reveal this effect, we introduce a belief-rollout diagnostic that traces how an agent's anticipated world state and future decisions evolve under alternative harnesses. We further separate immediate divergence introduced by the interface from divergence that develops over longer horizons. Across controlled coding tasks include HIBench-Code, SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, we find substantial cross-harness belief divergence that is often invisible from terminal success alone and can propagate into different downstream actions. Building on this observation, we introduce \textbf{Belief-Invariant World-Modeling (BIWM)}, which exposes evidence hidden or compressed by harness execution and aligns the resulting belief trajectories across different harnesses. Our findings reveal that harness design can systematically shape how software agents understand and act on their environments, providing practical guidance for building and evaluating more reliable coding agents.

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback
Authors: Yucheng Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Shusheng Yang, Jiakang Chen, Serena Yeung-Levy
First: 2026-10-09T17:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:58:15+00:00

Abstract
Multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) allow the evolving image to update internal text representations through joint attention. These representations then guide subsequent image updates, raising a question: does this feedback help correct deviations from the prompt, or preserve them? We find that image-to-text (I2T) feedback can preserve the objects and attributes already represented in the evolving image, including errors. Transplanting I2T messages can rescue failures or disrupt successes. At matched intervention strengths, suppressing the influence of I2T feedback improves generation, whereas opposite controls degrade it. These findings motivate Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), which suppresses the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens this feedback and weakens text-to-image conditioning. The in-place variant, iSFG, directly suppresses I2T feedback and strengthens text-to-image conditioning, requiring no extra model evaluations. Temporal interventions motivate running the SFG branch during the first 20% of image sampling steps, requiring about $1.2\times$ the base NFE without classifier-free guidance (CFG). Experiments across three image-generation backbones show gains in alignment and preference-model scores. On FLUX, adding SFG to CFG raises GenEval from $0.6661$ to $0.7479$; on SD3.5M, iSFG adds $+1.33$ HPSv2.1 over CFG at zero extra NFE. The control also transfers to text-to-video and image-to-video generation, improving VBench scores. Code is available at https://github.com/yyc1215lll-creator/SFG.

Evolving Hybrid Quantum-Classical Architectures for Image Classification
Authors: Devroop Kar, Daniel Krutz, Travis Desell
First: 2026-10-02T12:37:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:57:17+00:00
Comments: Under Review at The Fifteenth International Conference on Learning Representations 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hybrid quantum classical neural networks integrate parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with established deep learning architectures, but their performance depends strongly on the choice of quantum circuit architecture, a choice that remains largely manual. Most existing approaches rely on hand-designed or fixed circuit ansätze, requiring circuit structure, gate composition, and qubit connectivity to be specified in advance with no guarantee that they suit the task. This limitation is especially acute in image classification, where quantum circuits must transform features extracted by classical networks while remaining compact enough for practical training, requirements that generic, task-agnostic ansätze are unlikely to satisfy simultaneously. We extend EXAQC, an evolutionary framework for automated quantum circuit discovery, to image classification. EXAQC evolves PQCs as intermediate processing modules while retaining classical feature-extraction and prediction layers. On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, EXAQC achieves 98.42%, 90.62%, and 85.47% accuracy, respectively, while using comparable gate counts to other quantum architecture-search methods. Against classical networks, evolved hybrid models maintain comparable accuracy with substantially fewer trainable parameters, reaching 85.68% on CIFAR-10 with over 25$\times$ fewer parameters than a 10-layer CNN. Encoding choice also matters: rotation-based encodings (RX, RY, U3) outperform amplitude encoding by 22-25 points on CIFAR-10. These results demonstrate that automated circuit discovery yields compact quantum modules that can replace larger classical components in vision architectures while retaining competitive accuracy.

Hide and Seek: HIDE Concepts to SEEK explanations for black box models
Authors: Bhanu Tokas, Rahul Nair, Rockwell Jackson, Hannah Kerner
First: 2026-10-09T17:56:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:56:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Interpreting machine learning model predictions is a central challenge, particularly for black-box vision models. Concept-based attribution methods interpret models using human-understandable concepts. Existing concept-based methods can only interpret classification and regression models. They require access to model weights or large concept-annotated datasets, and most methods cannot provide image-level attribution. We propose Hide and Seek (HnS), a new method for concept-level attribution that does not require access to model weights or labeled concept datasets. HnS can interpret a wide range of vision models. Given a concept and image set, HnS retrieves concept-aligned images using a vision-language model, localizes the concept within each image, masks the localized region, and measures attribution from the change in the black-box model's output between the original and masked images. This enables faithful attribution by directly measuring the model's behavior and supporting local attributions. We validated HnS on CelebA and COCO-Stuff. Compared to existing methods, HnS showed better agreement with ground-truth attributions. We demonstrated the applicability of HnS on diverse tasks, including image captioning and satellite image segmentation, where no existing concept attribution method can be used. We provided our anonymous code in the supplementary material.

DEAF: A Benchmark for Diagnostic Evaluation of Acoustic Faithfulness in Audio Language Models
Authors: Jiaqi Xiong, Yunjia Qi, Qi Cao, Yu Zheng, Yutong Zhang, Ziteng Wang, Ruofan Liao, Weisheng Xu, Sichen Liu
First: 2026-03-17T15:52:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:56:35+00:00
Comments: 14 pages,6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent Audio Multimodal Large Language Models (Audio MLLMs) demonstrate impressive performance on speech benchmarks, yet it remains unclear whether these models genuinely process acoustic signals or rely on text-based semantic inference. To systematically study this question, we introduce DEAF (Diagnostic Evaluation of Acoustic Faithfulness), a benchmark of over 2,700 conflict stimuli spanning three acoustic dimensions: emotional prosody, background sounds, and speaker identity. Then, we design a controlled multi-level evaluation framework that progressively increases textual influence, ranging from semantic conflicts in the content to misleading prompts and their combination, allowing us to disentangle content-driven bias from prompt-induced sycophancy. We further introduce diagnostic metrics to quantify model reliance on textual cues over acoustic signals. Our evaluation of seven Audio MLLMs reveals a consistent pattern of text dominance: models are sensitive to acoustic variations, yet predictions are predominantly driven by textual inputs, revealing a gap between high performance on standard speech benchmarks and genuine acoustic understanding.

Abstract
While deep learning models for tabular data have recently received significant research attention, the choice of objective functions for regression tasks remains an understudied degree of freedom. Several lines of research offer alternatives to the standard Mean Squared Error (MSE), but differences in datasets, backbones, and evaluation protocols make their practical benefits difficult to compare. Overall, our results provide a modern empirical picture of regression objectives for tabular deep learning and highlight how differences in optimal stopping times across uncertainty regions can limit the effectiveness of global early stopping. Abstract
We study the last iterate of standard asynchronous Q-learning from a single trajectory of a finite Markov decision process. The trajectory follows a fixed behavior policy whose state--action chain is irreducible and may be periodic. For discount factor $γ$, write $H=(1-γ)^{-1}$, and let $μ_{\min}$ be the minimum stationary state--action probability and $\mathfrak h_\star$ the largest expected hitting time of the reverse state--action chain. We prove that zero-initialized Q-learning with a constant step size achieves sup-norm error at most $\varepsilon$ with high probability using $\widetilde{O}(H^4/(μ_{\min}\varepsilon^2)+\mathfrak h_\star)$ transitions, for $0<\varepsilon\le1$. The statistical term retains the quartic effective-horizon dependence of standard Q-learning, while initialization cost is additive in $\mathfrak h_\star$. For aperiodic behavior chains, a comparison with mixing time yields a transient term $\widetilde{O}(t_{\mathrm{mix}}/μ_{\min})$. An empirical state--action count rule selects a constant step size for a subsequent training run and attains the same rate without prior knowledge of model parameters, target accuracy, or confidence level. The output is the last training iterate, with both stages included in the sample count. The proof uses two deterministic Bellman envelopes to separate statistical error from fixed-policy initialization error and adapts reverse-time hitting-cost compensation from TD learning to state--action propagation. An exploration lower bound holds for arbitrary predictable step sizes. Together with the existing statistical lower bound and its extension to pilot-selected constant steps, it yields matching mean-square rates up to logarithmic factors on the stated large-sample parameter classes.

First: 2026-02-06T16:09:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:30:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Second-order momentum corrects the lag of momentum behind the current gradient with a Hessian-vector product (HVP), but existing corrections need either a Lipschitz Hessian or extra forward and backward passes at an auxiliary point. We show that randomizing the step length removes this trade-off: if the step is exponentially distributed with mean $η$, a Stein-type identity makes $η$ times one HVP at the new iterate an estimate of the gradient change that is unbiased in expectation over the step, and this HVP shares its forward pass with the next gradient. The resulting method, RanSOM-E, works with normalized, sign, and Muon-type steps, and a Beta-distributed variant, RanSOM-B, keeps Frank-Wolfe iterates feasible. For objectives with Lipschitz gradient and heavy-tailed gradient and Hessian noise with tail indices $p, q \in (1,2]$, RanSOM-E attains the optimal oracle-complexity exponents for every pair $(p,q)$, including $p \neq q$, without a Lipschitz Hessian or clipping ($\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-3})$ for $p = q = 2$). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, we detect no accuracy difference from existing second-order momentum methods, and its mean update time is lower than that of the auxiliary-point method.

Stochastic Compositional Optimization via Hybrid Momentum Frank--Wolfe
Authors: El Mahdi Chayti, Mohamad Fakhouri
First: 2026-05-14T19:20:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:27:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Stochastic compositional optimization minimizes $F(f(x), x)$ over a compact convex set when the inner map $f$ is available only through noisy samples. We propose Hybrid Momentum Stochastic Frank--Wolfe, a projection-free method for outer functions $F$ that are jointly convex and Lipschitz but not necessarily differentiable, such as maxima, CVaR, and norms. The method tracks the Jacobian and the value of $f$ with momentum, using a Taylor correction for the value, and passes the tracked linearization to a generalized linear minimization oracle. Under finite-variance noise, it attains an expected generalized Frank--Wolfe gap of order $K^{-1/4}$ for non-convex objectives and, when the linearized models minorize the objective, a last-iterate primal error of order $K^{-1/3}$. The analysis extends to heavy-tailed noise without clipping, with separate moment orders for values and Jacobians. With an exact oracle, the method reduces to the deterministic method of Vladarean et al. (2023).

Abstract
A memory that reproduces every current prediction may still have forgotten how to learn from the next observation. We characterize this distinction for exact cumulative learning and attention. For smooth convex residual losses with unrestricted real features and labels, a continuous recurrent state of fixed dimension exists exactly when the loss is polynomial. With ordinary, unweighted observations, degree $D$ requires precisely $r=\binom{d+D}{D}-1$ coordinates; even among histories with one fixed current predictor, exactly $r-d$ coordinates can remain necessary for continuation. Nonpolynomial losses have unbounded requirements on such equal-prediction histories. Bounded scalar logistic examples retain this obstruction after fixing any finite derivative jet. For ridge regression, the hidden evidence is the design matrix: the exact state costs $dp+d(d+1)/2$, versus $dp$ for the predictor. On a well-conditioned zero-prediction family, optimal $B$-bit compression has average one-update error of order $d^{-1}2^{-2B/s_d}$, $s_d=d(d+1)/2$, even with arbitrary randomized codes. Kernel attention gives a second exact closure classification. The results identify what future updates reveal, rather than proposing a new regression algorithm or asserting trained-model gains.

Abstract
Accurate detection of drivable area is essential for autonomous driving and advanced driver assistance systems. While state-of-the-art methods focus on general traversability estimation, they prove inadequate for snow-covered roads where following existing wheel tracks offers crucial advantages: improved traction, predictable vehicle dynamics, and verified path stability. To address this, we propose a low-cost, self-supervised approach detecting safety-critical wheel tracks using synchronized RGB-I (RGB + Long-Wave Infrared) imagery. Our method automatically generates training labels by projecting future wheel trajectories from recorded GNSS data. A neural network is designed to identify wheel tracks with the 4-channel RGB-I images from challenging, white-out road surfaces. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in visually monotonous and featureless adverse environment, offering a robust and cost-effective alternative for winter autonomy. The newly introduced ThermalTrack dataset is available at: https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/thermaltrack/.

Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization via Zeroth-Order Residual Tracking
Authors: El Mahdi Chayti, Alireza Mirrokni, Martin Jaggi
First: 2026-10-09T17:20:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:20:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many optimization problems provide direct access to only part of the gradient, while the remaining component must be inferred from function evaluations. This structure arises in performative prediction, performative reinforcement learning, and bilevel optimization with a black-box lower level. We introduce Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization (PISO), which combines the available gradient component with a running estimate of the residual. By centring each zeroth-order observation on this estimate, PISO makes the variance due to random probing depend on the tracking error rather than the residual's magnitude. This yields a randomized sketch iteration with an explicit contraction mechanism and a principled interpretation of momentum. For smooth nonconvex objectives, we establish convergence guarantees that depend on the residual's variation and initial magnitude, without requiring a uniform bound on the unavailable gradient component. With independently sampled function evaluations and Gaussian or spherical probes, PISO attains leading function-evaluation complexities of \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-6})\) under Lipschitz gradients and \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-5})\) under an additional Lipschitz-Hessian assumption. These rates require only one probe direction, two function samples, and one known-component sample per update. The analysis also quantifies how batching trades observations for fewer deployments. Across five settings, PISO variants improve on the tested baselines with comparable oracle access in three, reach a comparable solution faster in bilevel routing, and perform best among methods without response-model derivatives in performative reinforcement learning.

Abstract
NLP systems increasingly aggregate claims from retrieved passages, documents, and generated reasoning steps. Yet current factuality methods mostly verify claims individually or compare pairs of claims, leaving open the question of whether the entire aggregated claim set can be retained coherently. This misses a fundamental failure mode: claims may be locally plausible and pairwise compatible while still being jointly inconsistent. We formalize global consistency repair: given an inconsistent natural-language claim set, preserve the largest coherent subset while localizing the compact sources of inconsistency. We introduce QXR, an adaptive diagnosis-and-repair framework that treats an LLM as a noisy set-level consistency judge, localizes minimal conflicting subsets, and repairs the claim set by deleting a small hitting set of claims. Across LLM judges and benchmarks derived from VitaminC, FEVER, and conflicting-evidence RAG, QXR preserves substantially more valid claims than direct all-at-once judging while maintaining high precision. These results suggest that factuality in claim aggregation systems should be treated as a set-level repair problem, not only as local verification.
